Cornerstone · Marketing hiring

Where to hire an X (Twitter) marketer

Updated Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Discovery guide · Fee details link out to Platforms research

Organic posting, community replies, ghostwriting a founder voice, and paid ads on X are different jobs. “Grow my account” without a metric and a constraint set is how you buy vanity followers — or a banned login.

Match the job to the venue

Practical fitting guide (editorial judgment, not a ranking)
If you need…ConsiderBe careful when…
A packaged deliverable (calendar, audit, thread pack) Marketplace Gigs (e.g. Fiverr) with explicit outputs and revision rounds. Prepaid checkout. The Gig promises “10k real followers in 7 days.”
Ongoing ghostwriting / social ops Open marketplaces (e.g. Upwork) or direct hire after a paid trial week. Fund weekly/monthly milestones. You hand over the only owner login without 2FA and an access plan.
Voice-sensitive founder presence Direct hire from your network, niche communities, or referrals. Trial on a few posts before a retainer. You hire solely on follower count of the marketer’s personal account.
Paid ads strategy Specialists who show Ads Manager experience and creative testing plans — marketplace or agency. Separate budget for media spend. Someone bundles “ads + guaranteed ROI” with no tracking setup.

What to look for

  • Samples of accounts like yours (B2B SaaS ≠ meme page ≠ local restaurant).
  • A measurement plan: impressions, profile visits, link clicks, waitlist signups — not only follower deltas.
  • Clear stance on automation and engagement pods (you want policy-safe methods).
  • Writing skill — X marketing is mostly writing under constraints.
  • Process for approvals, crisis replies, and brand-safety stop rules.

Brief checklist (X/Twitter-specific)

Use the hiring brief template, then add:

  • Account URL, niche, and who the account represents (founder vs brand).
  • Goals for 30/90 days with numbers you can actually observe.
  • Topics to push, topics that are off-limits, competitors to watch.
  • Posting cadence, reply expectations, and timezone coverage.
  • Access model: which roles, which tools, password-manager sharing — never email passwords in plain text.
  • Disclosure rules for affiliate, paid partnerships, and AI-assisted drafts.
  • Success review date and kill criteria (what ends the engagement early).

Red flags

  • Guaranteed follower counts, “active user panels,” or purchased likes/retweets.
  • Requests for your password via chat or email instead of a proper access workflow.
  • No writing samples — only screenshots of analytics that could be anyone’s.
  • Pressure to disable 2FA or hand over the email inbox.
  • Strategies that clearly violate X rules (spam replies, misleading claims, botnets).
