Where to hire a copywriter (and how to brief them)

Updated Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Discovery guide · Fee details link out to Platforms research

Copywriters are not interchangeable with “content writers who SEO-stuff blogs.” Landing-page conversion copy, lifecycle email, and technical product UI strings are different jobs. Name the job before you pick a website.

Match the job to the venue

Practical fitting guide (editorial judgment, not a ranking)
If you need…ConsiderBe careful when…
A short packaged page or email set Marketplace Gigs (e.g. Fiverr) with word counts and revision rounds listed. You expect deep customer research or brand strategy inside a $50 Gig.
Samples and proposals from many writers Open marketplaces (e.g. Upwork). Ask for 2–3 relevant samples, not a free full draft. You hire on price per word alone — you will get generic filler.
Senior product / UX writing Curated networks or specialized communities; for screened matching see networks like Toptal when budget fits. Get a written quote. The work is a one-paragraph homepage tweak.
Ongoing brand voice Direct hire or retainer with a writer who already matched your voice in a paid trial piece. You skip a paid trial and commit to three months on a cold sample.

What to look for

  • Samples in your format (landing page ≠ blog ≠ ad).
  • Evidence they can write to a specific audience, not “everyone online.”
  • Comfort with your compliance constraints (claims, disclaimers, regulated niches).
  • Process for revisions: how many rounds, what “done” means.
  • Willingness to work from a brief and ask clarifying questions before drafting.

Brief checklist (copy-specific)

Use the hiring brief template, then add:

  • Audience, awareness stage, and the single primary CTA.
  • Offer, price, guarantees, and claims you can legally support.
  • Proof points: testimonials, metrics, demos (with permission notes).
  • Voice: 3 likes / 3 dislikes with URLs; words to avoid.
  • SEO constraints only if real (target phrase, must-use terms) — do not invent keyword stuffing goals.
  • Deliverable format (Google Doc, Figma text, CMS draft) and handoff owner.

Red flags

  • “Guaranteed #1 ranking” or guaranteed conversion rate lifts with no test design.
  • AI-only delivery sold as senior strategy with no editing pass disclosed.
  • Portfolio of only Lorem-looking fake brands with no live URLs.
  • Refusal to work in funded milestones for multi-page projects.
  • Copying competitor pages closely enough to create legal risk.
