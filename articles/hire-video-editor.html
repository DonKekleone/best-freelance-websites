Where to hire a video editor

Updated Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Discovery guide · Fee details link out to Platforms research

Short-form vertical hooks, long-form YouTube storytelling, and paid social ads are different crafts. Hire for the cut you need — and specify deliverable specs so “final file” means the same thing to both of you.

Match the job to the venue

Practical fitting guide (editorial judgment, not a ranking)
If you need…ConsiderBe careful when…
Templated short-form clips Marketplace Gigs (e.g. Fiverr) with clear length, captions, and revision counts. Prepaid orders reduce payment ambiguity. You need brand strategy, scripting, or filming — editing Gigs rarely include those.
Episode or campaign editing with proposals Open marketplaces (e.g. Upwork, Freelancer.com). Share sample cuts and fund milestones per episode/batch. Raw footage is a mess and you have not budgeted cleanup time.
Ongoing weekly channel volume Retainer / direct hire after a paid trial week. Marketplace contracts can still hold funds per batch. You commit to a year before seeing turnaround under real file sizes.
High-stakes brand film Curated networks or known studios; get a written quote and schedule. Screened networks (e.g. Toptal) may help for adjacent creative leads — ask; do not assume video is in every network’s vertical. Budget expects marketplace Gig pricing for a broadcast package.

What to look for

  • Portfolio cuts in your aspect ratio and pace (9:16 hooks ≠ 16:9 documentary).
  • Comfortable with your tooling (Premiere, Final Cut, CapCut, DaVinci) and file-transfer method.
  • Captioning / accessibility approach and language quality.
  • Music and footage licensing awareness — who buys the license?
  • Turnaround estimates that mention render + review time, not fantasy same-day for 4K multicam.

Brief checklist (video-specific)

Start from the hiring brief template, then add:

  • Platforms and aspect ratios (9:16, 1:1, 16:9) plus max length.
  • Raw file locations, naming, and proxy expectations.
  • Reference videos (links) with notes on pace, captions, transitions.
  • Brand kit: fonts, colors, lower-thirds, intro/outro.
  • Music: licensed library vs client-provided; mute-safe versions if needed.
  • Deliverables: resolution, codec, bitrate, thumbnail stills, caption files (SRT).
  • Revision rounds and what counts as a new cut vs a tweak.

Red flags

  • Editors who will only accept huge raw uploads over chat apps with no backup plan.
  • “Unlimited revisions” with no definition — usually means conflict later.
  • Using copyrighted commercial music without a license plan.
  • No watermarked draft before final unlock on large projects (and no funded milestone).
  • Portfolio that is only other people’s trending templates with no original assembly skill.
