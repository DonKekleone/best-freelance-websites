Where to hire a web developer (by job shape)

Updated Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Venue / discovery guide · Workflow lives on Hiring Hub · Fee details link out to Platforms research

This page is about where to look. Match the venue to the job shape (table below). For the full hiring workflow — specialty, brief, vetting, milestones, overseas questions and handoff — use how to hire a web developer on Freelance Hiring Hub. Cost context (not a rate card): web developer cost.
“Web developer” covers a WordPress plugin conflict, a marketing site on a static host, a Next.js product UI, and a full-stack SaaS rebuild. The venue should match risk and ambiguity — not the lowest hourly number on a search page.

Match the job to the venue

Practical fitting guide (editorial judgment, not a ranking)
If you need…ConsiderBe careful when…
A packaged landing page or theme setup Marketplace Gig platforms (e.g. Fiverr) with listed scope, revisions, and prepaid checkout. You need custom product logic, auth, or ongoing ownership of a private repo without a clear handoff plan.
Proposals from many specialists Open marketplaces (e.g. Upwork, Freelancer.com). Post stack, deadlines, and funded milestones. Your brief says “build my Uber for X” with no MVP slice — you will buy wishful thinking.
Senior screened talent quickly Curated networks (e.g. Toptal) with matching and trial periods. Ask for a written quote; there is no public rate card on Toptal’s FAQ. Budget is a few hundred dollars for a one-hour CSS tweak.
Someone you already trust Direct hire with a written brief + funded milestones (marketplace escrow or another escrow workflow). You skip funding and rely on “pay when done” for multi-week builds.

What to look for

  • Recent work in your actual stack (CMS vs React vs backend), not generic “full stack” badges.
  • A plan for environments: staging URL, deploy ownership, secrets handling.
  • Clear definition of done: pages, breakpoints, forms, analytics, redirects, accessibility basics.
  • Willingness to work in milestones (design freeze → build → QA → launch) with funds attached.
  • References or public repos that show maintainable code — not only pretty screenshots.

Brief checklist (web-specific)

Start from the reusable hiring brief template, then add:

  • Current site URL, hosting, DNS owner, and who can deploy.
  • Must-keep content, SEO URLs, and analytics IDs.
  • Integrations (CRM, payments, auth) and sandbox credentials process.
  • Browser/device targets and performance budget if you have one.
  • Post-launch support window (days of bugfix included).

Red flags

  • Quotes with no milestone plan and “50% upfront to my personal PayPal only.”
  • Promises of “Google page 1 in 7 days” bundled into a build quote.
  • No staging environment — edits straight on production.
  • Refusal to document admin logins, repo access, or domain ownership transfer.
  • Scope that grows weekly with no change-order process.
